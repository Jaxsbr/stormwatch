# Turtle casting study

Date: 29 September 2026. Status: pose research and proposed adaptation; not an approved animation.

## Primary reference

Fitec manufactures cast nets and publishes a [throwing tutorial](https://castnets.com/how-to-throw-a-cast-net/) with a [photographed instruction sheet](https://castnets.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/FITEC-CAST-NET-THROW-TIPS-ENGLISH.pdf). The sheet was inspected visually, including its load and release photographs.

- **Gather and load:** steps 2–4 bring the hanging net into the throwing hand and place another section of its weighted perimeter in the free hand. Hands are separated to keep the net partly open. This is a distributed load, not a small bundle pinched in one fist.
- **Turn and release:** step 5 specifies a quarter turn back, followed immediately by rotation toward the target and a slightly upward release. Its photographs show the torso participating, both arms reaching through the cast, and a changed stance.
- **Spread:** the release photograph shows a widening perimeter; step 6 expects a circular landing. The recognisable event is a broad opening net.
- **Follow-through:** the subsequent photograph shows the arms continuing toward the target after the net has left. A precise weight-transfer prescription is not stated; the stance change supports a visual inference of body weight accompanying the throw.

These observations describe Fitec's demonstrated technique, not every cast-net method. The [Pentair manual](https://pentairaes.com/media/docs/CN4-CN8-Instruction-Manual.pdf) was also requested but could not be retrieved; no findings depend on it.

## Proposed four poses for Stormwatch

The following is a **stylized adaptation**, not a literal recreation of human technique. Retain the original turtle's face, shell, scarf, body proportions, rendering style and planted stance. Work in one side view, mirrored for the other direction.

| Pose              | Body and hands                                                                                                                                                                                | Net silhouette                                                                                          |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Gather         | Both hands support the net low in front of the belly. Bent elbows point down/back naturally; hands and forearms stay attached to their sleeves.                                               | A substantial hanging crescent with a visible weighted rim, not a tiny cloth.                           |
| 2. Load           | A small knee dip and backward body shift show weight. Hands move back/up together toward the shell side while remaining in front of the torso silhouette. Head keeps attention on the target. | Net sags between separated hands; its lower edge lags behind the lift.                                  |
| 3. Cast           | Body rises/leans toward the target; both arms sweep forward and slightly upward. Leading hand extends, supporting hand opens. Shoulder motion leads the hands.                                | Net releases as a widening fan; the rim travels outward first and opens into an ellipse in perspective. |
| 4. Follow-through | Empty hands continue forward briefly, elbows soften, body settles into its original stance. No immediate reset at release.                                                                    | Net is fully separate from the hands and continues expanding as the projectile leaves.                  |

Use a slow, readable gather/load, a short accelerating cast, and a softer recovery. Exact timings should fit the existing firing interval; animation must not change simulation timing or target selection. A full character spin is unnecessary: suggest rotation through a restrained shoulder/body shift so the turtle remains identifiable in side view.

## Review before animation

1. Compare original art and all four poses at equal body height, including game scale.
2. Check sleeve openings, continuous shoulder–elbow–wrist anatomy, and shell occlusion. The existing reversed-looking elbow must not survive into the pose study.
3. Confirm two distinct hand contacts and a net large enough to read as a net. Keep it clear of the face and out of the shell.
4. Get the poses accepted before animating. Preserve the body artwork; only replace parts if the desired pose cannot be represented coherently with the existing art.
5. In playback, verify visible effort before release, a distinct opening/spread event, empty-hand follow-through, both mirrored directions and the actual battlefield scale.

The intended character is a steady, weighty turtle making a deliberate two-handed cast. The squirrel's straight brace/draw/snap should remain a different action.
