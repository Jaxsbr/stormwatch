# Skunk throwing-action references

Research date: 3 October 2026. Pose exploration only; none of these actions or payload appearances is owner-approved. Follow [the tower process](../../docs/TOWER-ANIMATION-PROCESS.md): coherent whole-character poses first, explicit approval before motion, separate battlefield approval later.

## A — Underhand lob / clay seedpod

**Primary observation:** Special Olympics teaches a smooth pendulum swing, release in front, and a forward/upward follow-through. Its skill checklist includes full arm extension after release and limited head movement. The guide distinguishes gentle pointing from a more forceful hitting action. [Special Olympics Bocce Coaching Guide, printed pp. 40–46 and 51](https://media.specialolympics.org/soi/files/sports/Bocce%2BCoaching%2BGuide.pdf).

**Proposed stylization:** Cup the seedpod below its centre in ready; carry it behind the near hip with a soft elbow; swing forward to a release just ahead of the hip; let the empty palm continue up before recovery. Raise the release into a visible lob rather than copying a low court roll. Keep both feet planted; use a slight chest lean and shoulder shift. This is the clearest restrained action and leaves Skunk's face unobstructed. It communicates a light, deliberate toss. The clay seedpod is a fictional visual proposal, not equipment derived from the source.

## B — Compact overhand / squat ceramic flask

**Primary observation:** Little League's five-step throw aligns the non-throwing shoulder to the receiver, separates the hands, raises the elbows into launch position, delivers with the throwing hand behind the ball, then continues body weight and arm toward the target. [Little League Coach Pitch practice sequence, throwing review and figures 3.1–3.5](https://www.littleleague.org/downloads/interactive-weeks-1-12/).

**Proposed stylization:** Show the flask in a cupped throwing hand beside the shoulder, with a bent elbow; bring hand above/forward of the shoulder for release; finish with the empty hand forward and down. Compress the full sporting step/torso rotation to a planted side-view weight shift. Keep the flask outside the face silhouette; do not draw a straight rigid arm for all four poses. This reads confident and brisk, but risks covering the face and needs more shoulder clearance. The corked flask is a fictional appearance proposal; source research concerns throwing only.

## C — Two-hand forward scoop / stitched leaf parcel

**Primary observation:** NSCA's modified horizontal scoop toss starts with a ball between the feet, held by both hands on opposite sides; the arms swing forward and release during an explosive jump. The article provides start, block-start and release photographs. [NSCA Personal Training Quarterly 1.1, “Land-Based Strength and Conditioning for Swimming,” printed p. 9](https://dxpprod.nsca.com/contentassets/574ab3a9e81e4063a759c38f29a717f8/land-based_strength_and_conditioning_-for_swimming.pdf).

**Proposed stylization:** Both palms cradle a parcel low in front of the belly; flex elbows and dip the torso slightly for preparation; extend both forearms forward/up together; open both palms at release and retain the forward reach briefly. Remove the source exercise's jump and floor pickup. Preserve the feet and baseline, with effort conveyed by shoulders and small body compression. This reads heavier and careful, but could resemble Turtle's two-hand casting; keep it low and compact instead of a large hoist/sweep. The leaf parcel is a fictional visual proposal.
