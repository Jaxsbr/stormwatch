# Research notes for MVP feedback

Date: 25 September 2026  
Purpose: collect first-party design and platform evidence that can inform the feedback on animation/readability, two-map progression, desktop/touch selection, and combat feel. This is research context, not a product decision or an independent acceptance review.

## Findings

### Readability of animated towers and enemies

George Fan’s GDC talk on *Plants vs. Zombies* describes teaching through play rather than text, spreading mechanics out, keeping words sparse, and using visuals to teach unit roles. The talk’s test is concrete: a player should be able to infer what a plant or enemy does from looking at it, or learn it after watching it perform once. [Fan, “How I Got My Mom to Play Through Plants vs. Zombies,” GDC 2012 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2012/slides/Design%20Track/Fan_George_How%20I%20Got.pdf)

Riot’s developers describe related production checks: distinct silhouettes help players recognize characters; animation that moves an actor outside its gameplay hitbox can confuse; added silhouette parts must be checked through the full animation for clipping; and the timing and spacing of poses can make an attack feel weighted. They also document cases where flashy effects, brightness, or smoke obscured gameplay information or implied a stronger attack than the underlying action. These are examples from a real-time action game, so they are useful review lenses rather than rules proven specifically for tower defense. [Riot, “Ask Riot: Let’s Talk Clarity”](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/ask-riot-let-s-talk-clarity/), [Riot, “Modernizing the Monk”](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-sg/news/dev/dev-modernizing-the-monk/)

**Design implications to investigate:** review each role at normal gameplay scale, not only as a large isolated rig. For enemies, check that facing, travel direction, feet/contact, and silhouette remain coherent through straight segments and turns. For each tower, check that its stable base, moving operator/weapon, aim, anticipation, attack origin, projectile, impact, and recovery agree with the tower’s actual rule. Check that idle motion does not read as an attack and that overlapping poses/effects do not hide an enemy’s role. These are review prompts derived from the sources, not findings about current Stormwatch assets.

Riot’s current first-party shader breakdown describes keeping characters visible against their environment, tuning clarity cues so they do not become distracting, and giving vision-blocking effects a clear gameplay boundary. A separate ability breakdown shows how distinct anticipation, strike, shockwave, and lingering-impact cues can communicate what happened. These examples support checking that effects remain readable and match the underlying action; they come from action games, not tower defense. [Riot Games, “VALORANT Shaders and Gameplay Clarity”](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/valorant-shaders-and-gameplay-clarity), [Riot, “A Storm is Brewing”](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-a-storm-is-brewing/).

Microsoft’s game accessibility guidance separately recommends strong contrast for important text and visual cues. Its guideline cites 4.5:1 for standard-sized gameplay information and 3:1 for large text/visual elements. The intended use here is as a contrast-check prompt for small health, targeting, and status cues against the dark woodland art; these figures are Microsoft’s guidance, not a claim that the project has completed an accessibility audit. [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 102: Contrast](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/102)

### Why continue after the first map?

The official *Plants vs. Zombies* manual documents progression through five ten-level stages that change the environment and constraints (day, night, pool, fog, roof). It also awards new plants as levels are completed, later asks players to choose a limited loadout, introduces an in-game plant/enemy almanac, and unlocks other modes at specific milestones. [EA/PopCap, *Plants vs. Zombies* readme](https://akamai.cdn.ea.com/eadownloads/u/f/manuals/GAME-PVZ/en_US_readme.html)

Ironhide’s official *Kingdom Rush* store description frames its 12-stage campaign as a journey to defend a kingdom, names multiple environments, and advertises tower specializations, distinct enemy abilities, bosses, and heroes. It also lists later mini-campaigns and bonus stages. This is a publisher description, not an independent evaluation of why players enjoyed it. [Ironhide Game Studio, *Kingdom Rush* on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/246420/Kingdom_Rush/)

**Design implications to investigate:** these examples connect several different reasons to continue: a legible next destination or threat, a change in the battle problem, newly available tactical options, and rewards that make a future choice different. A narrative reward, new enemy/tower behavior, or a progression reward could each contribute; the sources do not show that a permanent numerical power increase is necessary. For a two-map expedition, a useful playtest question is whether players can tell what they protected, why the next place matters, and what changes tactically after unlocking it. Which of those should carry Stormwatch’s motivation remains an owner decision.

### Selecting, placing, and deselecting with mouse and touch

Apple’s game design guidance says to support each platform’s expected interaction method and calls out control sizing and menu behavior when moving from pointer-based play to touch. It identifies touch as the default for iPhone, and keyboard/mouse or trackpad as the default for Mac. [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Designing for Games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games/)

Apple documents context menus as hidden until invoked: common triggers include touch-and-hold on iPhone/iPad or secondary click on a pointing device. It advises making context-menu actions available in the main interface too. For transient popovers, Apple says clicking/tapping outside or choosing an item generally dismisses them; a Close/Cancel/Done button is useful when it clarifies an outcome. A right-click/long-press can therefore be an optional shortcut, but these platform patterns do not make it a discoverable universal replacement for an obvious way out. [Apple HIG: Context Menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/context-menus/), [Apple HIG: Popovers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/popovers/)

Microsoft’s game UI guidance emphasizes consistent navigation and predictable ways back across screens. WCAG 2.2’s pointer-cancellation criterion also gives a useful placement check: avoid committing an action on pointer-down without a way to abort/undo, or provide an up-event reversal. This is relevant when evaluating whether selecting a build item, then tapping/clicking the field, makes accidental placement costly. [Xbox Accessibility Guideline 112: UI navigation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/112), [W3C WCAG 2.2, SC 2.5.2 Pointer Cancellation](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag/#pointer-cancellation)

Target-size references use different units and conformance levels: WCAG 2.2 AA specifies a 24×24 CSS-pixel minimum with exceptions, while its AAA enhanced target is 44×44 CSS pixels; Apple recommends 44×44 points; Android recommends at least 48×48 density-independent pixels. These numbers should not be treated as physically interchangeable. [W3C WCAG 2.2, SC 2.5.8 and 2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag/#target-size-minimum), [Apple UI Design Dos and Don’ts](https://developer.apple.com/design/tips/), [Android Developers: Make apps more accessible](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/views/apps-views)

**Design implications to investigate:** run the same short interaction tasks in desktop and touch modes: select an existing tower, inspect it, clear inspection, select a build item, cancel, place, and switch to another action. Record whether each mode offers a visible exit and a low-effort shortcut, how many actions it takes, and whether a mistaken action can be cancelled. Prototype a visible clear/deselect affordance alongside blank-field tap and a desktop secondary-click shortcut; compare discoverability and accidental actions instead of presuming one gesture works everywhere.

### Visual effects versus gameplay variety

The primary-source examples treat effects as signals about an action and its consequence, not as a substitute for a distinct mechanic. Riot’s clarity discussion describes preserving silhouette recognition through animation and adjusting effects or cues that distract from play; its effect breakdown shows timing and impact cues as part of making an action legible. George Fan’s GDC talk treats strategy, pacing, rewards, and unit design as distinct design concerns, and recommends letting players see units perform to understand them. [Riot, “Ask Riot: Let’s Talk Clarity”](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/ask-riot-let-s-talk-clarity/), [Riot Games, “VALORANT Shaders and Gameplay Clarity”](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/valorant-shaders-and-gameplay-clarity), [Riot, “A Storm is Brewing”](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-a-storm-is-brewing/), [Fan, GDC 2012 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2012/slides/Design%20Track/Fan_George_How%20I%20Got.pdf)

**Design implication to investigate:** separate a combat-feedback prototype from a strategy-variety prototype. The first can test whether anticipation, projectile travel, hit response, slow, defeat, and rewards are visible and satisfying at ordinary play speed. The second can test whether an encounter asks for a meaningful tower/upgrade/targeting choice and whether a different enemy mix or route changes that choice. Observe first-time players and capture the same mixed, busy wave in each variant. Effects may make existing decisions legible; they cannot by themselves demonstrate that the underlying decisions are engaging. That last sentence is an inference from the cited goals, not a measured claim about Stormwatch. [Riot, “Ask Riot: Let’s Talk Clarity”](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/ask-riot-let-s-talk-clarity/), [Riot Games, “VALORANT Shaders and Gameplay Clarity”](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/valorant-shaders-and-gameplay-clarity), [Riot, “A Storm is Brewing”](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-a-storm-is-brewing/), [Fan, GDC 2012 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2012/slides/Design%20Track/Fan_George_How%20I%20Got.pdf)

## Stormwatch material inspected

- Read `docs/SCOPE.md`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, `docs/GLOSSARY.md`, `docs/ROADMAP.md`, `docs/RESEARCH.md`, ADRs 004, 008, and 009, and `review/rubric.json`.
- Read the two encounter definitions and inspected the relevant selection/input paths in `src/main.ts` and `src/render/battlefield.ts`.
- The current acceptance contract specifies 44 CSS-pixel essential touch targets. That project requirement is aligned numerically with WCAG 2.2’s 44 CSS-pixel enhanced target; it should still be assessed separately against Apple points and Android density-independent pixels.
- The approved scope describes a short woodland expedition with two encounters, the second unlocked after the first, local stars and one alternative tactical-card unlock. The encounter data already has distinct titles and short fiction: Lantern Pass protects wagons on a forest route; Rainstone Crossing protects a storm-threatened river causeway. Wave names also provide a sequence of escalating events. This means story copy exists in the content; this research does not establish whether players notice it, believe it, or feel motivated by it.
- The inspected map-rendering code shows each node’s number, name, star state, and (when locked) a completion instruction. It does not put the full encounter description or wave titles on the map itself. This may help distinguish missing story content from story information that is only available deeper in the flow.
- In the inspected code path, a left-button pointer-up on the battlefield selects a tower or clears selection when it hits non-tower ground. The inspector and build/rescue modes each also have a visible close/cancel control; Escape clears those states. I found no secondary/right-button selection handler in the inspected battlefield input path. This is a source inspection, not a hands-on comparison of desktop and mobile usability.
- The project rubric already asks for normal-speed recordings of enemy walk/action/impact, grounded feet and stable identity; tower aim/windup/fire/recovery; and readable combat impacts/projectiles/slows. I read prior review reports as documentary context, but did not replay their video or audio evidence here. Accordingly, this note makes no new claim that any particular Stormwatch tower or enemy part is currently broken.

## Limits and open questions

The external sources are creator/developer guidance, first-party product descriptions, and platform/accessibility standards. They are reference points, not evidence that Stormwatch is fun or that one UI gesture will fit every player. WCAG web conformance is not the same thing as a game usability verdict. The named platform target sizes use different units. Current animation, combat feel, narrative motivation, and child/adult usability still need direct observation in the game at normal scale and on actual supported input devices.
